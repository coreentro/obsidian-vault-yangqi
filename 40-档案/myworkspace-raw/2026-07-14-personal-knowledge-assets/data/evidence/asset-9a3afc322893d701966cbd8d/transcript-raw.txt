z Catalysis
Nano-structured Magnesium Oxide as Efficient Recyclable
Catalyst for Knoevenagel and Claisen-Schmidt
Condensation Reactions
Biplab Roy +,[a] Anupam Singha Roy +,[a] Asit B. Panda, [b] Sk.Manirul Islam,* [a] and
Asoke P . Chattopadhyay*[a]
Nano-structured magnesium oxide was prepared by calcining
its hydromagnesite phase, obtained by simple precipitation us-
ing magnesium acetate and ammonium carbonate under am-
bient condition. This MgO was used as active and reusable cat-
alyst for the Knoevenagel condensation of aromatic aldehydes
with malononitrile and ethylcyanoacetate under solvent-free
conditions at room temperature. It also exhibits strong activity
in solvent-free base catalyzed Claisen-Schmidt condensation of
various substituted benzaldehydes with acetophenone. The ba-
sic sites of different strengths like surface hydroxyl groups, and
low coordination O
2/C0sites may be responsible for the above
catalytic activity. The experimental results showed that the
nano-MgO has high catalytic activity and can be recycled with-
out significant loss of its activity.
Introduction
Metal oxide based nanostructures have attracted much atten-
tion in the fields of catalysis, active adsorbents for gases, mag-
netism and microelectronics owing to their unique physical
and chemical properties. [1] The overall performances of nano-
catalysts depend on their size and shape. Nanocatalysis is inter-
esting in organic synthesis as it provides an alternative to ho-
mogeneous catalysis and also has the advantage of
requirement of minimum amount of the catalyst, reusability
and comparatively milder reaction conditions. Nano-structured
magnesium oxide is an efficient catalyst and has been used in a
variety of organic reactions, such as aldol reaction,
[2] asym-
metric Michael addition of malonates to enones, [3] asymmetric
Henry reaction, [4] benzylic C/C0H oxidation, [5] and synthesis of fla-
vanones.[6]
Chalcone is commonly synthesized via the Claisen-Schmidt
aldol condensation between ketone and aldehyde in basic or
acidic conditions. Chalcones have attracted attention due to
numerous biological applications. [7–11] The various types of cata-
lysts are used for the synthesis of chalcones including bases
such as NaOH, [9] KOH,[12] LiOH.H2O[13] and acids such as
BF3.OEt,[14] BiCl3,
[15] clay,[16] and sulfonic acid-functional ionic liq-
uids.[17] The Knoevenagel reaction of aldehydes with active
methylene compounds has been widely employed in the syn-
thesis of several fine chemicals, intermediates of anti-hyper-
tensive drugs and calcium antagonists. [18] This reaction is con-
ventionally catalyzed by alkali metal hydroxides or by organic
bases under homogeneous conditions. [19]
However some of these procedures suffer from many dis-
advantages such as long reactiontime, high temperature, for-
mation of side products and use of hazardous solvents. In this
respect, a heterogeneous catalyst is considered as an eco-
friendly alternative. Industry favors catalytic processes in view
of easy handling, simple work-up and regenerability. In this
way, as potential alternative, heterogeneous acid or basic cata-
lysts have received much attention for chalcone synthesis and
Knoevenagel condensation reaction.
[20–31]
Here, we report our results on the efficient synthesis of
chalcones by the Claisen-Schmidt condensation and Knoevena-
gel condensation reactions under solvent-free conditions cata-
lyzed by nanocrystalline MgO. The catalytic efficiency of nano-
crystalline MgO is discussed for the Claisen-Schmidt
condensation between substituted benzaldehydes and sub-
stituted acetophenones in the absence of solvent. We have
also studied the utilization of nanocrystalline MgO as an effi-
cient heterogeneous catalyst for the Knoevenagel con-
densation between substituted benzaldehydes and various ac-
tive methylene compounds at room temperature under solvent
free conditions. High activity was observed and the nano-
crystalline MgO catalyst was easily isolated from the reaction
mixture by simple filtration and reused without a significant
degradation in activity.

Results and Discussion
Characterization of nanocrystalline MgO
Magnesium Oxide can be prepared by the slight modification
of the reported method. [32] Magnesium acetate and ammonium
carbonates were mixed rapidly at room temperature to form
the white precipitate of basic hydrated magnesium carbonate.
Nanostructured magnesium oxide is obtained at ambient con-
dition after calcination. The powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD)
pattern (Supporting information, Figure S1) of the as synthe-
sized white precipitate can be indexed to hydromagnesite
(4MgCO
3.Mg(OH)2.4H2O) phase. The sharp lines in the PXRD
pattern (Figure 1) of the calcined sample indicate highly crystal-
line nature. The fresh and recycled MgO samples exhibit same
two sharp peaks at 42.7 o and 62.3o, besides a small hump at 37 o
which correspond to the (200), (220) and (111) reflections of cu-
bic MgO.
The microstructures of the synthesized MgO are presented
in Figure 2. The SEM image of MgO samples are shown in Fig-
ure 2(a). The synthesized MgO shows mixture of flakes and rod
needles morphology. Further structural characterization of the
MgO was confirmed by TEM. Figure 2(b) also reveals that the
synthesized nanostructured MgO is formed by the mixture of
flakes and rod needles shaped entities. FT-IR spectra of the as
synthesized and calcined samples are given in Figure S2 (Sup-
porting Information). The room temperature FT-IR spectrum of
as synthesized magnesium carbonate hydrates (Figure S2a)
shows characteristic symmetric stretching vibration of carbon-
ate ion at 1115 cm
/C01 and corresponding bending vibration
bands at 881, 854, and 802 cm /C01.[33] The asymmetric stretching
vibration of carbonate ion is split into three bands at 1524,
1477, and 1425 cm /C01 representing the existence of carbonate
ion as bi-carbonate species in the as synthesized material. A
very broad band at 990 cm /C01 corresponds to bending vibration
of adsorbed bi-carbonate. [34] Bands at 3554 and 3440 cm /C01 rep-
resent characteristic of water of crystallisation for hydro-
magnesite phase of MgO. Free O /C0H stretching vibration band
at 3650 cm /C01 and a broad band at 1630 cm /C01 due to O /C0H
bending mode of water are observed. [34] Thus, FT-IR studies
confirm hydromagnesite phase of the as synthesized materi-
al.[33–35] This is also supported by PXRD pattern. The calcined
MgO (Figure S2b) shows three distinct bands at 1652, 1460 and
1022 cm/C01. The bands at 1652 and 1460 cm /C01 can be assigned
to the anti-symmetric and symmetric stretching of -O-C =O moi-
ety of surface bicarbonate species whereas the band at
1022 cm/C01 may be correlated to the presence of unidentate
surface carbonate species. [34, 35] A broad band centred at
3450 cm/C01 corresponds to the stretching vibration of the coor-
dinated hydroxyl groups interacting with surface O 2/C0and a
band at 3705 cm /C01 is attributed to the mono-coordinated hy-
droxyl groups.[36] The FT-IR studies thus confirm the presence of
surface chemisorbed carbonate species, hydroxyl groups and
various O 2/C0ions. The diffuse reflectance spectrum is given in
Figure S3 (Supporting Information). Low coordinated oxide
sites and lattice defects, which is responsible for the high cata-
lytic activity, can be shown from this diffuse reflectance spec-
trum.The absorption band at 230 nm is related to the presence
of edge site oxide while the absorption at 270 nm can be cor-
related to the three coordinated oxide at the corner sites.
[37]
Catalytic activity
The catalytic activity of the nano-structured MgO was tested in
Claisen-Schmidt and Knoevenagel condensation reactions. In
the beginning, benzaldehyde and acetophenone were used as
model reactants for the Claisen-Schmidt condensation in order
to find the optimum reaction conditions (Scheme 1). Initially
the reaction was carried out in ethanol solvent. At first, opti-
mization of the amount of catalyst was carried out (Figure 3).
No reaction was observed in absence of nano-MgO. The re-
action was started from 0.005 g of catalyst; we observed that
the yield of the product gradually increased on increasing the
amount of catalyst. The maximum yield was obtained using
0.025 g of catalyst. No improvement in yield was observed with
further increase in amount of catalyst.

Recycling of nano-MgO
catalyst
Next, we investigated the pos-
sibility of recycling and reusing
the nano-MgO catalyst. The
catalyst was filtered from the
mixture, and directly reused for
the same reaction in the next
catalytic cycle. The result of re-
cycling is shown in T able 5. The
recyclability of the catalyst was
checked by carrying out re-
peated runs on the same batch
of the used nano-MgO catalyst
in Knoevenagel and Claisen-
Schimdt condensation re-
actions. Catalytic activity of the
magnesium oxide did not de-
crease significantly even after
five catalytic cycles. The SEM
(Figure S4, Supporting In-
formation) and XRD analysis
(Figure 1b) of the spent nanostructured MgO revealed that the
morphology and structure of the recovered MgO remain un-
altered during the recycling process which indicates that the
catalyst is stable during the reaction.

T o check the efficiency of the present method it was compared
with some reported methods. Base catalyzed condensation of
benzaldehyde with acetophenone or Knoevenagel con-

Conclusions
In conclusion, we have demonstrated an efficient, green, simple
nano-MgO catalyst and its use in Claisen-Schmidt and Knoeve-
nagel condensation of aldehydes with ketones and active
methylene compounds. Simple experimental work up, shorter
reaction time, solvent-free process,easy availability of the MgO
for catalyst preparation and excellent yields are the advantages
of the proposed method.The catalyst is easily recoverable, and
it can be recycled atleast five times without significant activity
loss.
