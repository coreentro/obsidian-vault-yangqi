abstract
Despite the existence of plentiful photocatalyst heterojunctions, their separation efﬁciency and charge
ﬂow precision remain low on account of lacking interfacial modulation. Herein, through a defect-
induced heterojunction constructing strategy, Ni
4Mo alloys were in-situ grown on the unsaturated
coordinated sulfur atoms of sulfur vacancies-rich ZCS (Sv-ZCS) via interfacial Ni-S covalent bonds. The
experimental and theoretical results reveal that these unsaturated sulfur atoms induced by sulfur vacan-
cies vastly facilitate to anchor more Ni-Mo nanoparticles and form abundant Ni-S covalent bonds, mean-
while, these sulfur vacancies could form dual internal electric ﬁeld (IEF) and work with Ni-S covalent
bonds as ‘‘Electron Bridge” to further accelerate photoelectrons transfer, as well as promote the activation
of water molecules and the desorption of hydrogen proton. Accordingly, the optimized Ni
4Mo/Sv-ZCS
composite achieves an improved photocatalytic hydrogen evolution (PHE) rate of 94.69 mmol h /C01 g/C01
without an evident decrease after 6 cycles of photocatalytic tests, which is 21.2 and 1.94 times higher
than those of Pt/ZCS and Ni 4Mo/ZCS, respectively. This tactic opens a new way for optimizing
ZnxCd1-xS-based heterojunctions by constructing sulfur vacancies and covalent bonds as ‘‘Electron
Bridge” to enhance the activity of PHE.

1. Introduction
The energy crisis and environmental pollution caused by fossil
fuels have been received more and more attention during these
years, thus developing an abundant and renewable energy source
is of great promise [1–3]. In consideration of high energy value
and environmental characters, high-efﬁciency and pollution-free
hydrogen is regarded as the most potential energy carrier. In vari-
ous synthetic methods for hydrogen, hydrogen generation from
water and seawater decomposition on photocatalysts with solar
energy is the most economical method to transform solar energy
to hydrogen energy [4–6].
In recent years, 1D Zn xCd1-xS (ZCS) nanorods have attracted
abundant attention on account of their high length-to-diameter
proportion for light capture and low reﬂectance due to light scat-
tering and trapping [7–11]. Nevertheless, a single ZCS photocata-
lyst reveals poor photocatalytic hydrogen evolution (PHE)
efﬁciency because of lacking active sites and its rapid recombina-
tion of photo-generated charges. For achieving a higher photocat-
alytic performance of ZCS, sulfur vacancies are induced into ZCS
to regulate photo-carrier migration [12–16]. However, these sulfur
vacancies conﬁned in the lattice will generate plentiful coordina-
tive unsaturation sulfur atoms, thus tremendously reduce the
semiconductor stability [17]. Recently, the construction of hetero-
junction in the vacancy sites has opened thoughts for improving
the metastability of defective photocatalysts [18]. Wang et al.
found that abundant coordinative unsaturation sulfur atoms in
Sv-ZnIn
2S4 could be used to anchor MoSe 2 to construct a robust
atomic-level interface via covalent bonds [19]. Under the intense
synergy among the Mo-S bonds, internal electric ﬁeld and sulfur
vacancies, Sv-ZnIn2S4/MoSe2 heterojunction enhances charge
transfer between components and achieves excellent PHE
performance.
To further improve the slow PHE dynamics, especially in alka-
line reaction liquid, active and serviceable cocatalysts are crucial.
As a standard PHE cocatalyst, the costly platinum (Pt) takes the
leading role in PHE activity, but its scarcity and high price seriously
limit wide application. Ni-based catalysts are the most widely used
electrocatalysts for hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) in the alka-
line condition. According to the previous research, several kinds of
metals have been found synergistic catalytic effects with Ni metal

strategy.
2. Experimental
2.1. Synthesis of sulfur vacancies-rich Zn xCd1-xS
ZnxCd1-xS (ZCS) sample was synthesized by a hydrothermal pro-
cess. Typically, 18 mmol of Cd(OAc) 2/C12H2O and 2 mmol of Zn
(OAc)2/C12H2O were added to 60 mL deionized water and ethylenedi-
amine. After that, 25 mmol of thioacetamide was uniformly dis-
persed into the above solution. Then, the mixed solution was
transferred into a Teﬂon-lined stainless autoclave and kept at
200 /C176C for 24 h. After cooling down to room temperature, these yel-
low precipitates were collected and dried in a vacuum oven.
Sulfur vacancies-rich ZCS (Sv-ZCS) material was synthesized via
a hydrothermal method [17]. Brieﬂy, the above ZCS (100 mg) was
dispersed into 20 mL of water and maintained in ultrasonic for
0.5 h, and then 5 mL of Hydrazine monohydrate (N 2H4∙H2O) was
dropped into the above mixture and stirred for 0.5 h. Simultane-
ously, this solution was maintained at 200 /C176C in stainless steel
autoclave for 6 h. Lastly, the precipitate was separated by centrifu-
gation, and washed with water, then dried at 50 /C176C for 10 h. The
acquired golden yellow sample was named Sv-ZCS.
2.2. Synthesis of Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS
100 mg of Sv-ZCS was dispersed into 60 mL water and main-
tained in ultrasonic for 1 h, and then NiMoO 4 cuboids were grown
on Sv-ZCS nanorods via a hydrothermal reaction [29,35]. Simulta-
neously, Ni(NO 3)2/C16H2O (0.1 mmol) and (NH 4)6Mo7O24/C14H2O
(0.025 mmol) were uniformly dispersed into the above solution
and maintained at 150 /C176C for 6 h. Then, the precipitate was col-
lected and dried at 60 /C176C for 10 h. Lastly, the obtained materials
were heated at 600 /C176C for 3 h in a H 2/Ar (15:85) atmosphere. The
resulting catalysts were named 0.1 Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS. And the 0.1 Ni 4-
Mo/ZCS composite was obtained by the same procedure. In this
work, 0.1 Ni 4Mo/ZCS and 0.1 Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS were defaulted to Ni 4-
Mo/ZCS and Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS. To optimize the PHE performance of
the heterojunctions, the samples with a different molar of Ni-
Mo-precursor were also synthesized by the same method and
denoted as 0.01 Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS, 0.05 Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS, and 0.2 Ni 4-
Mo/Sv-ZCS, respectively.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Morphology and structure characterizations
Herein, through a defect-induced heterojunction synthesizing
strategy, the Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS composite with sulfur vacancies and
interfacial Ni-S covalent bonds was constructed. Firstly, with the
addition of N 2H4∙H2O solution, abundant sulfur vacancies were
fabricated in ZCS nanorods [36], meanwhile, sulfur atoms in the
adjacent position of surface sulfur vacancies became unsaturated
coordinated sulfur atoms ( Fig. S1), where these unsaturated sulfur
atoms will spontaneously anchor Ni 2+ cations and contribute to the
construction of interfacial Ni-S bonds [17]. Subsequently, many
NiMoO4 NPs were synthesized with Ni 2+ and Mo 7O24
6/C0ions via a
hydrothermal reaction. At last, the above precursor was reduced
into Ni 4Mo alloy under high temperature reducing conditions
[29]. Based on the above analysis, a brief compositing process of
Ni4Mo/Sv-ZCS heterojunction is illustrated in Scheme 1 .
The microstructure and morphology information of Ni 4Mo/ZCS
and Ni4Mo/Sv-ZCS heterojunctions were detected by the transmis-
sion electron microscopy (TEM) and high-resolution transmission
electron microscopy (HRTEM) ( Fig. 1 ). Obviously, compared with
Ni4Mo/ZCS (Fig. 1a), Ni4Mo/Sv-ZCS photocatalyst shows that more
Ni4Mo NPs are in-situ grown on Sv-ZCS nanorods due to the
anchoring effects of unsaturated sulfur atoms ( Fig. 1 b and c) [37].
And it is also certiﬁed by the inductively coupled plasma optical
emission spectrometer (ICP-OES) results, which the mass ratio of
Ni-Mo alloy in Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS sample is about two times of that
of Ni 4Mo/ZCS heterojunction, indicating that unsaturated sulfur
atoms in Sv-ZCS component vastly facilitate to anchor more Ni-
Mo NPs (Table 1). Furtherly, a closer look at Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS sample
(Fig. 1d) could simply conﬁrms that the lattice fringes with a spac-
ing of 0.325 and 0.204 nm are indexed to (101) plane of ZCS and
the (121) plane of Ni 4Mo alloy, respectively [28,38,39]. Fig. S2
and Fig. 1 (e) are the EDS plot and element mapping of Ni 4Mo/Sv-
ZCS, as exhibited, the distribution of Zn, Cd, S, Ni, Mo elements
are highly homogeneous.
The crystal structures were examined by the X-ray diffraction
(XRD). As for ZCS component, the peaks at 24.8 /C176, 26.45 /C176, 28.2 /C176,
36.6/C176, 43.8 /C176, 47.8 /C176and 51.9 /C176are assigned to the (100), (002),
(101), (102), (110), (103) and (112) facets of wurtzite ZCS (JCPDS
No. 65-3414) [8,38]. As for the rest of photocatalysts in Fig. 1 (f),
all the diffraction peaks of heterojunctions are identical to the
hexagonal phase of ZCS, while the peaks of Ni, Mo and Ni 4Mo alloy
are not observed in any heterojunctions because the mass ratios of
Ni, Mo and Ni 4Mo are highly low, and these signals are also possi-
bly overlayed with more intense peaks of ZCS [40]. To conﬁrm the
successful synthesis of metallic Ni, Mo and Ni 4Mo by our method,
the phase identiﬁcations of the above metals were also measured
by XRD. As observed ( Fig. S3 ), the sharp XRD peaks at 44.5 /C176,
51.8/C176and 76.3 /C176are indexed to Ni metal (JCPDS, No. 70-1849),
and the peaks located at 40.6 /C176, 58.7 /C176and 73.8 /C176originate from
metallic Mo (JCPDS, No. 71-4645) [28]. Additionally, the XRD pat-
tern of Ni 4Mo alloy has three discernible diffraction peaks (JCPDS
No. 04-0850), which is the same with the previous research
[29,41,42]. These results evidently testiﬁed that the Ni, Mo and
Ni4Mo NPs could be successfully synthesized by our method.

3.2. Photocatalytic hydrogen evolution
The PHE efﬁciencies of all as-prepared photocatalysts
(k /C21420 nm) were tested by on-line photocatalytic analysis system
in Na 2S-Na2SO3 solution (Labsolar IIIAG, Beijing Perfectlight).
Clearly, the ZCS photocatalyst displays a poor PHE efﬁciency of
about only 4.47 mmol h /C01 g/C01, whereas the Sv-ZCS presents a
greatly improved PHE rate of 9.85 mmol h /C01 g/C01 on account of
the accelerated separation of photogenerated carrier induced by
sulfur vacancies as the electron traps ( Fig. 3 a and b) [13,14,49].
Furtherly, the introductions of Ni, Mo and Ni 4Mo cocatalysts dis-
tinctly give rise to the PHE efﬁciencies. Speciﬁcally, after coupling
with Ni4Mo alloy, the PHE rate of Ni 4Mo/ZCS sample reaches a high
PHE efﬁciency of 48.79 mmol h /C01 g/C01 than those of Ni/ZCS
(36.4 mmol h /C01 g/C01) and Mo/ZCS (27.7 mmol h /C01 g/C01) samples,
which means the intense synergy of alloying Ni with Mo signiﬁ-
cantly enhances the PHE performance [50]. Especially, in contrast
with other samples, the PHE rate of Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS presents the
highest PHE efﬁciency, which is 1.94 times higher than that of Ni 4-
Mo/ZCS, implying that the construction of Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS hetero-
junction tremendously accelerate the interfacial charge transfer
(Fig. 3 b and Fig. S5 ). Besides, Fig. 3 (c) shows the wavelength
dependent hydrogen production rate of Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS, which
was carried out via an identical process of PHE, except for the

3.3. Photocatalytic mechanism
Fig. 4 (a) is the UV–vis diffuse reﬂectance spectra (UV–vis DRS)
of ZCS, Sv-ZCS, Ni/ZCS, Mo/ZCS, Ni 4Mo/ZCS and Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS. As
observed, the light absorption intensity of Sv-ZCS component is
higher than that of pristine ZCS, which means that the sulfur
vacancies could enhance visible-light absorption and further
reduce the band gap ( Fig. S6 a) [12]. In addition, after the in-situ
growth of Ni 4Mo alloy on Sv-ZCS, the light absorption of Ni 4Mo/
Sv-ZCS distinctly increases across the visible region compared to
Sv-ZCS. This improved visible-light absorption is beneﬁcial for
the effective separation of photoinduced charges, and in favor of
the improvement of photocatalysis in the visible light region. Fur-
therly, the ﬂat band potential is measured by Mott-Schottky plots
according to the previous research [51]. As shown in Fig. S8 , the
ZCS component has the lower ECB and EVB as well as a wider band-
gap of n-type feature [52]. With the doping of sulfur vacancies, the
ECB and EVB just reveal more negative with decreased bandgap
[40,53]. Thus, according to the previous research, the electrons of
vacancies-rich part would spontaneously ﬂow into ZCS part due
to the higher Fermi level of vacancies-rich part under darkness,
then an IEF will be built from vacancies-rich part to ZCS part [54].
As displayed in PL spectra ( Fig. 4b), the pure ZCS exhibits a big-
gish emission peak under 400 nm excitation wavelength, implying
the severe recombination of photoinduced charges. In contrast
with single ZCS, the emission signal of Sv-ZCS sample slightly
reduces, which can attribute to that sulfur vacancies as electrons
trap facilitate the separation of photoinduced electron-hole pairs
[49,55]. Notably, the PL intensity of Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS photocatalyst
is extinct in contrast with Sv-ZCS, conﬁrming the helpful role of
Ni-Mo alloy for inhibiting the recombination of photo-charges in
Sv-ZCS. Additionally, as shown in the photocurrent response
(Fig. 4c), all tested photocatalysts reveal the light-response charac-
teristic under visible irradiation. Different from the weak pho-
tocurrent responses of other samples, the highest current of
Ni
4Mo/Sv-ZCS can be discovered in Fig. 4 (c), which testify to the
efﬁcient separation of photo-generated charges. Fig. 4 (d) is the
electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS). Clearly, the semidi-
ameter of Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS is tremendously decreased than other
samples, which means that the construction of sulfur vacancies
and Ni-S bonds decreases the interfacial charge transfer resistance,
thus the recombination of photo-generated carriers is inhibited
[56].

played, the electron cloud density reveals obvious local distribu-
tion among Ni
4Mo alloy and Sv-ZCS, which directly indicates the
intense bonding interaction among Ni and S atoms. Furtherly, it
is worth noting that Ni 4Mo alloy is principally overlayed by yellow
cloud, while Sv-ZCS model is mainly covered by blue cloud, imply-
ing that the electrons in Sv-ZCS are moved to Ni 4Mo alloy through
the Ni-S covalent bonds.
To probe into the intrinsic mechanism of the enhanced PHE per-
formance in alkaline solutions, the H 2O dissociation and the hydro-
gen proton (H*) desorption were simulated by DFT calculations
(Fig. 5 f). As displayed, the H 2O dissociation free energy ( DGH-OH*)
on ZCS is approximately 0.8 eV, indicating that the H 2O dissocia-
tion on single ZCS component demands a high activation energy,
thus contributing to sluggish PHE kinetics of ZCS photocatalyst
[60]. Notably, the DGH-OH* of Ni4Mo/Sv-ZCS (/C00.55 eV) is far lower
in contrast with Ni 4Mo/ZCS (/C00.31 eV), which means that the exis-
tence of unique sulfur vacancies in Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS can effectively
contribute to the activation of the water molecule, which is funda-
mentally favorable to PHE activity [61]. Furtherly, compared with
ZCS, the free energy of hydrogen absorption ( DGH*)o fN i 4Mo/ZCS
(/C00.47 eV) and Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS ( /C00.5 eV) are far closer to zero. It
reveals that the addition of Ni-Mo alloy could be helpful to reduce
H* into H 2. As a result, Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS heterojunction contributes to
the H2O activation and improve the desorption of H* for the reduc-
tion of H 2.
Based on experimental and theoretical analysis, a probable
reaction route on Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS could be proposed. Firstly, due
to the difference in work functions between Sv-ZCS (5.327 eV)
and Ni 4Mo alloy (4.635 eV) ( Fig. 5 c and d), the electrons of Ni 4Mo
component will ﬂow to Sv-ZCS [3]. Consequently, the charge redis-
tribution resulted in the band edge downward bending of Sv-ZCS
and the formation of an IEF from Ni 4Mo to Sv-ZCS [38]. As dis-
cussed above, Sv-ZCS component has an IEF from vacancies-rich
part to ZCS part [45]. Subsequently, under visible irradiation, Ni 4-
Mo/Sv-ZCS composite is excited for the separation of photo-
induced electrons and holes in the CB and VB of Sv-ZCS component,
respectively. Driven by dual IEF in the same direction, abundant
photoelectrons in Sv-ZCS would be transferred to Ni 4Mo alloy
through the Schottky barrier via Ni-S covalent bonds [45]. Then,
the H-OH bonds of water molecules on Ni 4Mo alloy could be effec-
tively active by these photoelectrons to produce the hydrogen pro-
ton (H*). Accordingly, the H* intermediates absorbed on Ni 4Mo
alloy are reduced into hydrogen by its enriched photoelectrons.
In addition, the photo-holes and oxidizing free radicals are rapidly
consumed by the hole scavenger, ultimately efﬁciently enhancing
the photostability of Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS and leading to the great
improvement of PHE activity. In this system, through defect engi-
neering, the constructed sulfur vacancies and Ni-S covalent bonds
could neatly serve as ‘‘Electron Bridge” to regulate the unidirec-
tional migration of photoelectrons and the adsorption energies of
various molecules [40,62].
4. Conclusions
In general, the unsaturated coordinated sulfur atoms in ZCS
induced by sulfur vacancies were used to in-situ grow Ni-Mo NPs
for synthesizing an extremely effective and sturdy Ni 4Mo/Sv-ZCS
composite via interfacial Ni-S covalent bonds for PHE activity. It
is found that sulfur vacancies in Sv-ZCS component form a dual-
IEF and further work with Ni-S covalent bonds as ‘‘Electron Bridge”
to tremendously regulate electrons transfer. The above defect
engineering contributes to the effective PHE performance of
Ni4Mo/Sv-ZCS Schottky heterojunction. Especially, the optimized
Ni4Mo/Sv-ZCS photocatalyst reveals the highest PHE efﬁciency of
94.69 mmol h /C01 g/C01. This work provides helpful enlightenment
on regulating Schottky heterojunction by defect engineering to sig-
niﬁcantly improve the PHE performance.
