RESEARCH ARTICLE
Significant removal of ammonia nitrogen in low concentration
from aqueous solution at low pH by advanced air stripping
Shaorong An1 & Qiang Jin 1
Received: 29 August 2020 / Accepted: 22 February 2021
# The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer-Verlag GmbH, DE part of Springer Nature 2021
Abstract
In recent years, the excess discharge of ammonia nitrogen from wastewater into surface water has been regulated by more
stringent standard. The air stripping method is successfully used to treatment of high-concentration ammonia nitrogen; however,
alkali will be added to keep pH more than 10, which is costly and not environment-friendly operation. In this study, an advanced
air stripping (AAS) based on foam separation of removing ammonia nitrogen in low concentration from aqueous solution at low
pH was proposed. The effect of conditions such as air flow rate, temperature, SDS dosage, coexisting ionic strength, pH, and
initial ammonia nitrogen concentration on the removal efficiency was studied. The advanced air stripping exhibited favorable
removal efficiency for NH4
+-N in low concentration from aqueous solution (20 mg·L−1) with a broad range of low pH 3.0 – 9.0.
Besides, for strongly alkaline (pH=11.0) solution, the advanced air stripping can alleviate the decrease of pH to some extent and
keep ammonia nitrogen stripping out continuously based on equilibrium shift between NH4
+ and NH3. A microcalorimeter was
applied to demonstrate the interaction between the negatively charged hydrophilic groups of SDS and NH 4
+ ions, helping to
understand the mechanisms more clearly. The simple operation and the satisfactory removal efficiency could imply that the
advanced air stripping is a promising technology for minimizing low-concentration NH4
+-N.
Keywords Ammonia nitrogen . Low concentration . Low pH . Advanced air stripping . Foam separation
Introduction
Nitrogen (N) is an essential element and nutrient for terrestrial
and aquatic ecosystems. However, excess accumulation of
nitrogen compounds in the water contributes to eutrophica-
tion; consequently over-growing algae would cause reduction
of the dissolved oxygen, which is toxic to aquatic organisms
(Guo 2007; Liu et al. 2013). Ammonia nitrogen (NH4
+-N) is
the primary nitrogen-containing pollutants in the hydrosphere
in recent years (Guaya et al. 2015), especially municipal and
industrial drainage (Zhang et al.2019). Therefore, many coun-
tries require NH 4
+-N to be removed from wastewater earlier
than discharge. For example, the Chinese municipal wastewa-
ter safe discharge level of NH4
+-N is restricted to 5 mg N L −1
in the majority of the wastewater treatment plants (WWTPs)
for class I effluent (GB 18918-2002).
Nowadays, various methods have been applied to the treat-
ment of ammonia nitrogen wastewater, which include biolog-
ical treatments (Pollice et al. 2002), break point chlorination
(Paul et al. 2017), chemical precipitation (Huang et al. 2015),
membrane filtration (Bódalo et al. 2005), ion-exchange ad-
sorption (Feng and Sun 2015), air stripping (Li et al. 2020),
and so on. Nevertheless, biodegradation is limited by the long
retention time and large spatial requirements, break point
chlorination is constrained by high cost and secondary envi-
ronmental effects because of numerous toxic and harmful dis-
infection byproducts, and chemical precipitation and mem-
brane filtration are also limited by high cost.
Compared to the abovementioned technologies, the air
stripping method is successfully used as pretreatment of
high-concentration ammonia nitrogen wastewater due to

mentioned above, we proposed an advanced air stripping
(AAS) method. Currently, no related studies can be found
on the AAS for ammonia nitrogen wastewater treatment.
Only a few researchers used foam fractionation to remove fine
particles in the recirculation aquaculture system (RAS), find-
ing that a little ammonia nitrogen was also removed at the
same time, which may be caused by the presence of surface
active substances such as proteins (Sun and Wu 2008). As
commonly anionic surfactant in all kinds of detergents and
decontamination powders, sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) al-
ways exists in the sewage discharge. So we verified the effect
of AAS by adding SDS on ammonia nitrogen removal. And it
was proved feasible to remove ammonia nitrogen in prelimi-
nary experiments (Text S1,F i g .S1,T a b l eS1).
The objectives of this study are (1) to investigate this
method’s performance for removing ammonia nitrogen in
aqueous solution under key operating conditions (air flow
rate, temperature, SDS concen tration, additional ionic
strength, and initial ammonia nitrogen concentration); (2) to
confirm the superiority of the AAS over the CAS in the re-
moval of low-concentration ammonia nitrogen at low pH; and
(3) to reveal the possible mechanism via reaction heat detec-
tion. This work can provide a valuable information for poten-
tial application on industrial ammonia wastewater at low con-
centration and low pH.
Materials and methods
Materials
(NH4)2SO4 (A.R.) was purchased from Sinopharm Chemical
Reagent Co., Ltd., China. Sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) was
purchased from Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology
Co., Ltd. Sodium hydroxide and sulfuric acid obtained from
the same company were used to adjust the pH of the solution.
Other reagents used for the measurement of ammonia nitrogen
were supplied from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd.,
China. Distilled water was operated in all experiments.
Experimental setup
The advanced air stripping column was manufactured from
transparent polymethyl methacrylate with a height of
500 mm and an inner diameter of 70 mm. An air pump and
a gas rotameter were used to supply and adjust the air flow,
and air passed through a gas distributor with a pore size of
approximately 50 μm located at the bottom of the column.
Then, small bubbles were created in the homogeneous liquid
solution. Based on favorable thermodynamics, the pollutants
to be removed can be ion-exchanged with the surfactant
adsorbed on the gas-liquid surface. A connecting tube was
installed at the top of the column, and the foam can flood
directly into a collection vessel. The schematic diagram of
advanced air stripping was illustrated in Fig. 1.

The experimental data were obtained at least three replicates,

Effects of temperature on the removal efficiencies of
ammonia nitrogen
Temperature is one of the most vital factors affecting ammo-
nia stripping efficiency (Tao and Ukwuani 2015). For the
purpose of exploring its effect on ammonia removal, changes
in temperature with the extent of 25~55 °C were tested. Initial
ammonia concentration, initial pH of solution, and air flow
rate were basically maintained constant at 20 mg L
−1, 7.0,
and 300 mL min −1 L−1 during the experiments, respectively.
The effects of temperature on ammonia nitrogen removal by
air stripping are presented in Fig. 3 and Table 2.R e s u l t s
shown in Fig. 3a from the experiment indicated that there
was no removal on a whole in the temperature range of
25 °C to 55 °C, and it was not feasible to remove low-
concentration ammonia nitrogen by CAS at an initial pH of
7.0. Contrary to the traditional methods, Fig.3b shows that the
AAS could significantly reduce the low concentration of am-
monia nitrogen concentration. The increase of temperature
was beneficial for the improvement of ammonia nitrogen re-
moval rate to a certain extent, but the removal efficiency was
not always higher at higher temperatures according to Table2.
When the temperature changed from 25 °C to 55 °C, the
removal efficiency (R, %) of ammonia nitrogen for 420 min
of aeration was 70.05%, 83.36%, 81.33%, and 77.45%, re-
spectively. At the same time, the enrichment ratio (E) contin-
ued to increase, from 5.64 to 9.40.
For CAS, the elevated temperature will not only enhance
the ammonia diffusion coefficient in liquid and gas film, but
also will change the liquid viscosity and surface tension and
the liquid-gas distribution ratio of ammonia. In other words,
the increasing temperature has a very significant impact on
desorption of ammonia from bulk liquid owing to decreasing
dissolution. However, ammonia nitrogen basically remained
in the form of NH
4
+ and a much lower NH3/NH4
+ at pH = 7.0
in this study. Therefore, it had no effect on blowing off am-
monia nitrogen, which was also the bottleneck of current re-
search. Generally, it is necessary to add alkali into the reaction
for achieving a high free ammonia fraction in actual wastewa-
ter treatment (Zhang et al. 2012a).
In contrast, for AAS, as the temperature rose from 25 °C to
35 °C, the surface tension decreased, resulting in the increase
of foaming ability and removal rate (Fig.3b). However, when
the temperature increased to 55 °C, the foam became extreme-
ly unstable and easily coalesced. Eventually, the rates of foam

Effects of SDS concentration on the removal
efficiencies of ammonia nitrogen
In view of the fact that the conventional air stripping had no
effect on low-concentration ammonia nitrogen at pH 7.0, for
separating NH 4
+-N from aqueous solution by advanced air
stripping, SDS played a dual role as both a collector and a
surfactant. The influences of SDS concentration on the remov-
al efficiencies of NH4
+-N were examined under the operation
of initial liquid volume 500 mL, air flow rate 300 mL min −1
L−1, and pH 7.0. SDS dosage ranged from 0.0 to 0.8 g.
As seen from Fig. 4 and Table 3, the increase in SDS
concentration in terms of removal efficiency (R, %) also en-
hanced. As the SDS solution in different concentrations being
studied, the presence or absence of the foam layer was obvi-
ously different throughout the process with each other. Since
the foam layer disappeared in 180 min and 300 min when the
SDS dosage was 0.2 g and 0.4 g, respectively, the ammonia
nitrogen concentration in the remaining solution was no lon-
ger reduced by foam separation, whereas for SDS dosage of
0.6 g and 0.8 g, the foam layer lasted for 420 min, so the
ammonia nitrogen concentration in the residual solution al-
ways dropped. Accordingly there was not much difference
in the final removal efficiency of ammonia nitrogen, as shown
in Table 3. With the increase of SDS concentration, the vis-
cosity of the solution increased, while the surface tension de-
creased. So, the foam became fine and uniform (Table 3),
making the foam more stable and difficult to drain, thus the
water content of the foam increased (Li et al. 2015). Thus a
strong SDS dosage of 0.8 g would result in the lowest surface
tension and largest liquid holdup of the rising foam, leading to
the lowest enrichment rate and the highest removal efficiency.
Taking into consideration of the cost and the performance, it
was clearly found that the dosage of SDS was not the larger
the better. So, the dosage of SDS was all 0.6 g in next study.

with the capture agent as a composite form in the foam liquid.

concentration surfactants from non-surface-active substances
in defoaming liquid (Guo et al. 2016). According to the pre-

Effects of additional ionic strength on the removal
efficiencies of ammonia nitrogen
It has been frequently reported that coexisting ions in aqueous
solution affect foam separation (Zhang et al.2011). Thus, it is
necessary to explore the effect of common cations in actual
wastewater, such as Na
+, on the removal of NH 4
+-N from
aqueous phase. The effect of ionic strength, adjusted with
sodium sulfate, on removal of ammonia nitrogen by AAS
was studied. The dosage of Na 2SO4 varied from 0 to 0.079
g, which were 0.5, 1, and 2 times the molar concentration of
NH4
+-N, respectively. The results are expressed in Fig. 6 and
Table 4. The AAS was negatively impacted by the coexisting
ion. When the dosage of Na 2SO4 was 0.079 g, the removal
rate of ammonia nitrogen was 74.18%, which was significant-
ly lower than the 83.36% of the blank control group. This
phenomenon can be explained as follows.
i. The competition between coexisting cation and NH 4
+ on
the adsorption of SDS (carriers).
ii. The change of the charge on the hydrophilic group of
SDS. Once the coexisting cations were adsorbed, the at-
traction of the anionic groups of the SDS would be weak-
ened (Mahmoud and Lazaridis2015). Then, the ammonia
nitrogen removal rate decreased.
On the other hand, with the improvement of additional ion
concentration, the foaming bubble size showed a decreasing
trend (Table 4). This is because when Na
+ ions with opposite
charges neutralize the charge of the SDS polar groups, elec-
trostatic repulsion on the gas-liquid interface reduced, thereby
increasing the SDS in foam in terface adsorption quantity
(Zhao 1984). Furthermore, the foam stability and the foam
yield increased, and the enrichment ratio reduced.
Although cations coexisting in water had an adverse effect
on the detachment of NH 4
+-N, it should be mentioned that
only limited decrease of the removal efficiencies occurred
when there were fewer cations coexisting in water.
Therefore, AAS is still a promising process for removing
NH
4
+-N in low concentration from wastewater in practical
application.
Effects of initial ammonia nitrogen concentration on
the removal efficiencies
To investigate the suitable ammonia nitrogen concentrations
for AAS and CAS, we chose 20~100 mg L−1 as low ammonia
nitrogen concentrations in the study. Initial pH of solution, air
flow rate, temperature, and SDS dosage were basically main-
tained constant at 7.0, 300 mL min −1 L−1, 35°C, and 0.6 g
during the experiments, respectively. The results of air strip-
ping are presented in Fig. 7 and Table 5. For CAS, with the
initial NH 4
+-N concentrations ranging from 20 mg L −1 to
100 mg L −1, there were no changes of ammonia nitrogen
concentration in the aqueous solution for 420 min as seen
from Fig. 7a, which indicated that CAS basically had no effect
on the elimination of ammonia nitrogen in neutral water.

crocalorimeter. Then, thermodynamic analysis is performed to

Conclusions
The removal of ammonia nitrogen from aqueous solution at
low pH by advanced air stripping was investigated in this paper.
It was found that it is feasible to remove ammonia nitrogen by
AAS. The effects of air flow rate, temperature, SDS concentra-
tion, coexisting ionic strength, pH, and initial ammonia nitro-
gen concentration on the removal rate were explored. For AAS,
the higher the gas flow, temperature, and SDS dosage were, not
necessarily the more efficient the removal was, because the
stability of the foam layer and the liquid-holding capacity must
also be considered comprehensively. Besides, the initial
